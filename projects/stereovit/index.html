AbstractRead the abstract

Stereo cameras give robots metric geometry from passive, cheap sensors, and stereo input has been shown to improve manipulation policies over monocular RGB. Yet unlike monocular vision, stereo lacks a widely adopted feature backbone for robot learning: leading stereo models rely on cost volumes, feature warping, and iterative refinement, so their geometry emerges over many refinement steps, leaving intermediate features less suited for direct reuse. Inspired by recent evidence that these inductive biases are not strictly necessary for accuracy, we present StereoViT, a streamlined stereo vision transformer that provides both accurate disparity and transferable stereo features for robotic manipulation. StereoViT first uses a depth-pretrained ViT to encode each view, and then a transformer decoder to relate the stereo pair with attentions, predicting disparity in a single feed-forward pass. With one checkpoint, StereoViT ranks first on KITTI 2012 and 2015 (all metrics), ETH3D (BP-0.5), Middlebury (BP-4), and Spring (all metrics) and is 1.5–4.8× faster than competing methods. Its depth yields the highest grasp success among stereo methods, while its frozen stereo features match the strongest diffusion policy baselines in clean scenes and achieve much higher success rates under domain randomization on the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark. Real-world experiments further confirm these gains.
